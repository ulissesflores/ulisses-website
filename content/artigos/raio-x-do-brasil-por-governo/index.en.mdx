The image that circulated can't answer it: 29 of its 57 numbers do not come out of the public data. Seventeen of those don't come out under any calculation I tested, and the image leaves out five governments.

The image reached me on WhatsApp on October 19, 2026. The oldest post I found is on X, from October 18, with a caption that, translated from the Portuguese, reads "I asked ChatGPT which was Brazil's best government?"

With the public data, only in part. In an index written before the calculation, only the first and last positions separate when the weights change. The middle of the table (Dilma, Temer, Bolsonaro) does not separate, and the index describes the country, not the merit of the government. Failing to reproduce does not prove the image lied: under another poverty line or another window, 12 of the 29 fall within tolerance.

The gold hatched blocks are the governments the infographic does not have.

**Between 1964 and 2026, the image covers 8 of 13 heads of government and leaves out 34.3% of the months.**

The five omitted are Castelo Branco, Geisel, Figueiredo, Sarney and Collor: 257.6 months out of 750.1. The dictatorship header says 1967-1974, but the GDP uses 1968-1973 and inflation, 1967-1973. Also left out is everything beyond the scoreboard's seven indicators: the [repository](https://github.com/ulissesflores/brazil-governments-factcheck) publishes 101 series across 19 domains, and the image carries none of them.

> **Methodological note.** The criterion is in code (`verdict.py`) and was fixed before the result. *Reproduces*: rounded to the printed decimals, it matches. *Approximates*: within 0.3 percentage point (the difference between percentages) on rows in %, or 5% relative. *Does not reproduce*: beyond that. *No series*: no official series covers the window.
>
> One specification per indicator; the rest is sensitivity. GDP: IBGE (Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics) and Ipeadata. IPCA (the official inflation index): series 433 of the SGS, the Central Bank's catalog. Unemployment: PME (Monthly Employment Survey) and PNAD Contínua (Continuous National Household Sample Survey). Real wage, December to December: Ipea (Institute for Applied Economic Research). Poverty and Gini (an index from 0 to 1: the closer to 1, the more unequal the income): the World Bank's PIP (Poverty and Inequality Platform), at US$6.85/day in PPP (purchasing power parity). Life expectancy: UN, via the World Bank.
>
> Each download enters `manifest.jsonl` with the hash (a file's fingerprint) of the previous line, and the SHA-256 seal (the algorithm behind that fingerprint) is checked with `python3 make_provenance.py --verify`. Nothing is estimated, imputed (filled in with an assigned value) or interpolated (drawn as a line between two known points); a series with a method break is not spliced. The preregistration (a rule sealed before the calculation) has 13 dated amendments; before downloading, only the list of what the image omits was sealed, and the specifications came after an exploratory pass. DOI is the permanent identifier of the deposit. The same design is in [how I checked the 2026 election polls](/artigos/pesquisas-eleitorais-2026).

## Since the Real Plan, only the first and last positions separate

By the close of the country-conditions index, since the Real Plan (the stabilization plan that created the real currency): 6th FHC (33.6; keeps its place in 100% of the 10,000 draws of weights); 5th Dilma (57.5; 61%); 4th Temer (58.8; 72%); 3rd Bolsonaro (63.9; 64%); 2nd Lula 1-2 (75.4; 81%); 1st Lula 3 (83.0; 88%). The order is that of each term's last year in the index, not of the government's merit. Dilma, Temer and Bolsonaro do not separate: the bands from the 5th to the 95th percentile overlap.

Two more cautions, beyond the index describing the country's conditions and not merit. First, ten items are long trends, such as life expectancy, infant mortality and GDP per capita, which rise almost every year and favor whoever governs later.

Second, on two V-Dem (Varieties of Democracy) items the 2024 value is itself the "best" benchmark of 1995-2024. They are judicial constraints on the executive (v2x_jucon), at 0.954, and political corruption (v2x_corr), at 0.438. Part of the jump in Institutions under Lula 3 is the ceiling of the scale.

There are 35 items in 12 areas, from 0 (worst of 1995-2024) to 100 (best). The direction comes from a Q1 article with a DOI (Q1 is the top quarter of the SJR 2024, a journal ranking). Homicide, inflation and debt are inverted. Equal weight per area is a stated judgment; the adjustment by effective importance did not converge.

The 10,000 draws (seed 20261020) give the band from the 5th to the 95th percentile. Each term is a trading candlestick (a *candle*, as in price charts). FHC opens in 1995, with no 1994; Temer opens at 71.8 (2015), not at the 57.5 of 2016, because he took office in August.

The editor asked for one more indicator after the index was published. It is v2x_freexp_altinf, *Freedom of Expression and Alternative Sources of Information*. It is context on the terminal page and does not score (EMENDA-13, an amendment to the preregistration, October 25, 2026). Point estimate: 0.924 in 1995, 0.931 in 2010, 0.809 in 2016, 0.704 in 2022, 0.923 in 2024. Liberal democracy already contains freedom of expression.

The editor's hypothesis is in EMENDA-12, October 25, 2026, registered before searching. For him, the Central Bank's autonomy and the legal spending limits "in a way keep things from getting worse". This article neither asserts nor tests it: only peer-reviewed studies speak.

## The Real Plan goes into the filter and comes out with zero

This layer does not measure whether a policy worked. An article gets in if it has a DOI registered at Crossref, is from a Q1 journal, has Brazil in the title or abstract, and names an identification design in the abstract.

Of 7,487 papers, 2,909 left without a DOI and 2,625 for being outside the SJR 2024. Other cuts removed the rest, leaving 43 studies for the 27 episodes. Anyone who merely mentions the design passes: cutting by hand would be my judgment.

Round 10 had 6,497 papers and 38 included; four institutional episodes came in on October 25, 2026 by dated amendment. Counting studies per term would measure elapsed years and declared episodes, never the quality of the government; I don't count.

## The headline got the balance right and swapped the name of the asset

On October 21 and 22, 2026, six outlets published the same trio, US$407.1 billion, US$402 billion and US$5.1 billion (Estadão/Broadcast, NC News, Brasil em Folhas, Cardinot, Revista Oeste and O Antagonista, the latter citing Estadão). Repetition is not a second round of reporting.

**The US$5.1 billion of June 2026 is the official figure of series 3685; the US$402 billion is not the cash reserves.**

The Central Bank has two gross measures, and I never add them. The restricted one, series 3684, since 1972, gathers loans, securities abroad and trade credit. It is the one in the headlines: US$407.1 billion in June 2026, the highest nominal value (in current dollars).

The BPM6 (Balance of Payments Manual, 6th edition), series 25563, since 2002, adds to that total transactions between companies of the same group and domestic securities held by non-residents. In June 2026 it stands at US$869.5 billion, which the Central Bank calls "Brazil's external debt".

The net one, series 3685, is still published. It is column M of the Central Bank's Table 31. It results from the restricted gross figure minus reserves, credits abroad and banks' holdings (what Brazilian banks have deposited abroad). Negative means creditor.

In June 2026 it stands at +5.1 billion, the first positive since September 2007 (17.0 billion); in December 2007 it was at -11.9 billion. The headline got the number right, and the "first time in 19 years" too.

The US$402 billion, however, are not the cash figure of series 3546, which stood at 367.6 billion in June 2026: they join reserves, credits and holdings, which the Central Bank subtracts. The story says the credits are already included.

The ratio to GDP, series 11409, stopped in February 2026 and I do not extend it: 46.3% in December 1984, 32.7% in December 2002, -0.9% in September 2025. The story says the net debt "is below 0.3% of GDP"; the calculation is the story's, and I did not run it.

## What I would do with this

I would not answer which government was best with this image: it does not have that answer. The question in the title only gets answered after saying what you want to know: conditions in the country or merit of the government.

**Before forwarding the next number, I would demand three names: the series, the window and the calculation.**

Without the name of the calculation (average, real December-to-December change or the level of one year), the cell is not a fact. If you want to redo it:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/ulissesflores/brazil-governments-factcheck
cd brazil-governments-factcheck
python3 run_all.py
```

The Python 3.11 standard library or newer is enough; the run ends with `run_all: OK`. If it comes out different, send it to me: I update and credit.

*Checked on October 25, 2026 against the sealed repository (DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23604103](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23604103)), the methodology and the series of the Central Bank, IBGE, Ipea, the World Bank, OpenAlex, Crossref and SJR.
